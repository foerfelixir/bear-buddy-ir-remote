Reverse engineering

Traced from the physical board

These notes accompany a schematic reverse-engineered by physically tracing the PCB lines. It was not supplied by the manufacturer.

Verify before using

The traced schematic is an evidence-backed reconstruction, not an authoritative manufacturing drawing. Check continuity, supply voltage, polarity, and every net on your own board before powering or fabricating hardware.

Target MCU

The remote uses the WCH CH32V003J4M6 in the 20-pin J package: 2 KB SRAM, 16 KB flash, RV32EC core, up to 48 MHz, and a 3.3 V supply rail. This firmware runs from HSI at 24 MHz divided by 16 to a 1.5 MHz SysTick tick.

Buttons

Each switch shorts its MCU input to ground when pressed. The firmware enables the internal pull-up and configures EXTI on a falling edge.

Priority is SW1 > SW2 > SW3. Releasing the active button sends nothing; release of any non-active button does not interrupt it.

IR output

PA2 drives the base of an NPN transistor through 1 kΩ. The transistor switches the IR LED active-low with a 47 Ω series resistor.

TIM1 generates the 38 kHz carrier, so the CPU does not interrupt to produce each carrier cycle.

Power and MCU rail

The original board steps a CR2032 cell to approximately 3.2 V using an RT9193 LDO. The MCU side includes 1 µF bulk and 100 nF decoupling.

Use a stable supply. Weak cells and IR LED current peaks cause erratic programming and resets.
